      METHOD & PITCH · SMART CAMPUS ANALYTICS

      How the score is built, and why it matters.

      The two deliverables the challenge asks for besides the prototype: a short note on the Student Success Score, and the pitch in six slides. Everything below is computed from the active dataset.

      Open the campus dashboardOpen the score explorer
      PART 1 · SCORING NOTE
      1. DATA INTEGRATION

      Seven source tables, one join key (student_id). Each export is cleaned before it is trusted: duplicates removed, out-of-range marks clipped, attendance capped at recorded sessions, names normalised, orphan rows dropped and logged. A missing record stays missing; it is never turned into a zero.

      2. STUDENT SUCCESS SCORE

      Score = Σ indicator (0 to 100) × weight. Weights reflect what the campus can act on: academics first, then presence and engagement.

      • Academic average35%mean of all scored assessments, 0 to 100
      • Attendance15%attended ÷ recorded sessions, whole year
      • LMS activity15%70% assignment completion + 30% login frequency (5/week = 100)
      • Campus engagement15%events ×8 + clubs ×12 + hackathons ×15 + certifications ×10, capped at 100
      • Skills assessments10%mean of technical and soft-skill scores
      • Student satisfaction10%survey rating ÷ 5 × 100; feedback a student submits never changes the score

      Missing indicators are excluded and the remaining weights rescaled to 100%, so a student with no LMS record is scored on the other five, not penalised.

      3. PLACEMENT READINESS, SCORED SEPARATELY
      • Aptitude30%latest aptitude test
      • Coding30%latest coding assessment
      • Mock interview25%panel score
      • Technical skills + certifications15%technical ×0.8 + certifications ×5, capped at 100
      Under 50Placement support needed
      50 to 64Build readiness
      65 and abovePlacement ready

      Kept apart from the success score so that a strong academic record cannot hide weak placement readiness, which is exactly the segment the challenge calls out.

      4. AT-RISK IDENTIFICATION: RULES + PREDICTION

      Rules (explainable, today). Declining results (semester average falls 8+ points), low subject average (below 55 over 3+ records), repeated topic difficulty (two low scores on the same topic), missing assessments and low attendance as context. Two academic signals = Priority review; one = Support recommended; context only = Monitor.

      Prediction (early warning, next semester). A transparent logistic model: logit = −0.35 + 0.075·(62 − academic) + 0.045·(75 − attendance) + 0.02·(60 − LMS) + 0.6·backlogs + 0.12·(−trajectory slope) + 0.25·missing assessments. Probability ≥ 60% = high, ≥ 35% = elevated. A trajectory falling 4+ points per semester raises an early warning on its own. Coefficients are hand-set for the demo; an institution fits them on its own outcome history.

      5. SEGMENTS (BONUS)
      6. LIMITATIONS, STATED ON PURPOSE
      • 1Fictional data. Every figure is generated or imported; nothing here is a real student.
      • 2Signals, not labels. Flags are reviewable prompts for a human conversation and never shown to students as judgements.
      • 3Observed change ≠ caused change. The intervention tracker reports before/after averages without causal claims.
      • 4Subject-level marks and topic tags only. Question-level and sub-skill analysis would need question-level data.
      • 5The predictive weights are illustrative. Fitting and validating them is the first task once real outcome data exists.
